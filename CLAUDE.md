# Luma — Project Context for Agents

HackGT 13 hackathon project. Keep this file current: when you make a decision, add a dependency, change the architecture, or finish/start a major piece, update the relevant section below (especially **Status** and **Decisions Log**) in the same change. Keep it short. Delete what's stale.

## The Idea

The app is called **Luma** (renamed from Tiny Town; the demo seed email domain `tinytown-demo.example.com` and the `tt-theme` localStorage key keep the old name so existing accounts and settings still work).

An isometric town where each character is one of the user's real friends. The town reflects what's actually happening in friends' lives (from signals they opt in to share), and characters act on their own inside the town to surface connections, which become real-world suggestions the humans can accept.

## Goals

- **Strengthen real friendships.** The point is to get people talking and hanging out more in real life, not to keep them in the app. Every feature should push toward a real-world connection: a message, a check-in, a plan.
- **Help people notice each other.** Show who's having a hard week, who has good news, and who shares an interest, so friends can show up for each other at the right moment.
- **Bridge people who should know each other.** Point out overlaps between friends, and between friend groups, that people wouldn't have spotted themselves.
- **Make keeping in touch easy and fun.** Turn "we should hang out" into something concrete through quests and suggested plans, with less of the work of organizing.
- **Earn trust.** Friends share only what they opt in to, the town never makes things up about real people, and humans stay in control of anything that reaches someone.

When choosing between features, pick whichever does more for real-world connection.

## Architecture

1. **Town brain**: `backend/loops/brain_loop.py` finds towns with signals newer than their last `brain_runs` row (`db.towns_with_unprocessed_signals`), then `run_brain_for_town` calls Gemini Flash via OpenRouter. Output is Pydantic-validated and visibility-filtered, then written to `town_members` (mood/activity/state) and `brain_runs.output` (facts + news). `events` are user calendars, not brain output.
2. **Character agents**: `agent_loop` claims due `agents` rows, and only the process holding `loop_leases` name `agent` does. Town time is the `town_clock` row (slider scrubs it, Live, Play (1 game minute per second) and Fast resume from that hour; a restart does not add process uptime). Where someone stands is written only by `snap_town_to_clock` / `snap_member_to_clock` (`plan_clock_placement`): idle means x/y is that door. Every `agents` update sets a new `written_by` or Postgres rejects it. Trigger `agents_follow_calendar` (`20260926000015`) re-plans every position change in SQL (`calendar_placement`, a mirror of `current_trip`) at `target.clock_at` and rejects it if the calendar, the door, or the town clock disagree. Change the SQL and `calendar_drive.py` together. The agent does not commit a calendar position. A failed model call does not change `action` while a calendar trip owns the character. Chat bubbles go in `agent_actions.details.lines`.
3. **Plan drafting (stub, no model yet)**: when every participant accepts, `events.status` goes `suggested` → `scheduled` and `GET /events/{id}` includes a drafted `plan`. A participant approves it via `POST /events/{id}/approve` → `confirmed`. The draft is recomputed on read; persisting it needs a migration.

## Hard Rules

- **Agents only use real facts.** Never invent feelings, events, or relationships about real people.
- **Humans approve anything that leaves the town.** Agents never send messages as the user in real channels.
- **Fixed action menu.** New actions get added to the menu deliberately.
- **Pitch honestly.** Only call something an "agent" if a model is actually making decisions.
- **LLM output never hits Postgres raw.** Brain and agents go through `backend/agent/validate.py` (Brain also `backend/brain/visibility.py`). Those loops are the only writers to `brain_runs`, town-member AI fields (`mood`/`activity`/`state`), `agents.action`/`target`, and `agent_actions`. Exceptions: a person moving their own character via `POST /towns/{id}/members/me/move` (`target.by = "user"`), and calendar travel (`backend/calendar_drive.py`) which follows `events` times. The brain does not write `events`.
- **`brain_runs` is readable by every town member.** Store only signal/member ids in `input` and only fully visible facts in `output`.

## Tech Stack

- Rendering: Three.js with an orthographic camera, which reads as isometric. Roads, greenery and props use SimplePoly City (Unity Asset Store) converted to GLB in `town/assets/simplepoly-city/` by `town/tools/import-unitypackage.mjs`, placed at its native scale (road tile = 20 units). Buildings mix SimplePoly with Kenney City Kit Commercial/Suburban (fit to the lot). Street props also from Coding Creature City Props 1 (CC0, `assets/city-props/`). Characters are Kenney Mini Characters. 3D code in `town/`. (2D Phaser prototype was tried and dropped.)
- Two web folders, served together from the repo root by `python3 serve.py` (only `frontend/` and `town/`, never `.env`; no caching):
  - `frontend/` = the account app (no 3D): `index.html` + `app/app.css`, `app/app.js` (sign in → pick username → tabs Feed `#/`, Friends `#/friends`, Inbox `#/inbox`, Profile `#/profile`; settings and help from the avatar menu), `app/ui.js`, `app/wheel.js`, `app/character-preview.js` (the one 3D bit, loads three and models from `town/`). `shared/` is used by both folders: `session.js` (Supabase client, URL/key from `GET /demo/config`, authed `api()`), `config.js` (backend URL), `colors.js`. supabase-js is vendored in `frontend/lib/supabase.js`.
  - `town/` = the 3D map: `index.html` (`/town/?town=<id>`, redirects to `/frontend/` when signed out; if the backend is down the scripted town still runs) + `town.css`, `js/` modules: `main.js` (boot + frame loop), `layout.js` (which town, grid, places, friends), `assets.js` (model catalog), `stage.js` (renderer, scene, camera, lights), `models.js`, `city.js`, `people.js` (characters + walking), `camera.js` (focus + follow), `effects.js` (incl. house moods), `demo.js`, `occlusion.js`, `sky.js` (time, weather, night lights), `heavens.js` (sun, moon with phase, stars; off unless `?sky=1`), `banner.js` (town name on a banner towed by a plane, low past the far corner), `mine.js` (your bubble, house mood, mailboxes), `buildings.js` (place card), `realtime.js` + `townsync.js` (backend bridges). Three.js vendored in `town/lib/` (plus FontLoader/TextGeometry and the MIT-style helvetiker bold font in `lib/fonts/`, unused since the banner replaced the 3D-letter sign), models in `town/assets/`, asset import tool in `town/tools/`.
- Backend: Python 3.11+ / FastAPI, `supabase-py` (secret key). **All LLM calls go through `backend/llm.complete()`** (check `llm.available()` first); new AI features must too. Provider: xAI Grok when `XAI_API_KEY` is set (`XAI_MODEL` = `grok-4.6` for brain/town generation; `XAI_FAST_MODEL` = `grok-4.20-0309-non-reasoning` for `AGENT_MODEL` calls: agents, chats, post ideas; `XAI_REASONING_EFFORT=low`), otherwise OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, old name `GEMINI_MODEL_KEY`; Town Brain `google/gemini-2.5-flash`, agents `google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite`). `LLM_PROVIDER=xai|openrouter` forces one. Two asyncio loops; no Redis/Celery.
- Database: Supabase (Postgres + Auth + Realtime).

## Database

Hackathon-simple on purpose: 12 tables (10 core + friends/invites), plus `town_clock` and `loop_leases` so every process shares one clock and one agent writer. Add more only when a feature needs them. Schema lives in [supabase/migrations/](supabase/migrations/). Schema changes go in a **new** migration file. The Supabase GitHub integration applies new migrations on `main` automatically, so **never change the schema by hand in the dashboard**. Preview locally with `supabase db push --db-url "$SUPABASE_DB_URL" --dry-run`.

**Tables**
- `profiles`: one per user, auto-created on signup. Unique `username` (how people find each other; set via `PATCH /me`), avatar (`{character, color}`), `interests` (text array), `bio` (≤160 chars) and a free/busy `status` with `status_until` (always expires; `backend/status.py`), both from migration `20260926000011`.
- `towns`: name, `invite_code`, `tiles` and `map`. `tiles[y][x]` (any rectangular size up to 64x64) says what's on each tile: `road`, `park`, `pond`, `tree`, `path` (walkable grass with a light brown track), `stadium`, `farm`, `home`, `driveway`, `yard`, `lot` (frontend fills it procedurally), decorative scenes `garden`/`picnic`/`plaza`/`patio`, and symmetric-park pieces `oak`/`fountain`/`water` (small pond)/`bench-n|s|e|w` (bench's long side faces that direction), or an explicit model key (asset path without `assets/`/`.glb`). `map` holds named places `{places: {cafe: {name, model, tile, door}}}` plus scenery: `landmarks`, and `background_homes` `{color, homes: [{model, house: [x, y]}]}` (unowned one-tile houses; the tile holds the model key and the frontend paints their roofs `color`; no plot, driveway, fence, car or name tag). The city is exactly the grid; nothing is drawn past its edges. See `backend/town_map.py`; `python3 -m backend.seed_demo_map` writes the frontend's hard-coded city into `DEMO_TOWN_ID`.
- `town_members`: who's in which town, their house tile (`house_x/house_y`) and `home` details (`{model, driveway, door, block, name?, mood?}`), and what the town hall AI currently shows for them (`mood`, `activity`, `state` JSON). Person-set, expiring: `bubble` `{text, set_at, until}` (3h, migration `20260926000020`) and `home.mood` `{kind, set_at, until}` (3h, like free/busy; kinds in `backend/house.py` MOODS = `effects.js` HOUSE_MOODS).
- `friendships`: in-town agent relationship, one row per pair (`user_a < user_b`) with `path_score`. Not the same as account friends.
- `signals`: raw inputs from users (`source` = manual, calendar, music, ...).
- `brain_runs`: each town hall AI run's `input` and `output`.
- `events`: things people share from their real life (class, work, gym, dinner) with `start_at`/`end_at`/`kind`/`building_id`/`travel_minutes`. Type `personal`. `building_id` is always set (home → `house:{user_id}`, campus → `library`, else `downtown`). News/quests are no longer stored here.
- `event_participants`: who is going to that calendar item.
- `agents`: one character per town member (position, current `action`, `target`, `next_decision_at`). Auto-created when a member joins.
- `agent_actions`: log of every agent decision; chat bubbles go in `details.lines`.
- `calendar_connections`: one per person who connected Google Calendar (refresh token, last sync/error). Backend-only: RLS on, no policies. Synced events land in `events` (`type=personal`, one copy per town) marked `imported_from='google'` / `imported_for=<user>` so each sync replaces them. Each copy goes to the town place its Google title/location names (`building_id`, title "At <place>"), or home (title "Busy") when nothing matches. The Google title and location are never stored.
- `friend_requests`: account-level friends, one row per pair either direction. `accepted` = friends; unfriending deletes the row.
- `town_invites`: town creator invites a friend; accepting adds a `town_members` row. Invite codes still work too.
- `mailbox_messages`: notes left in a townmate's 3D mailbox (`to_user`, `from_user`, `text` ≤500, `read_at`), migration `20260926000020`. Backend-only (RLS on, no policies). Leaving a town deletes your mailbox.
- `post_reactions` (one emoji per person per post, from a fixed set) and `post_comments`: responses to posts (`signals` with `value.audience`), migration `20260926000018`. Backend-only (RLS on, no policies); the API checks you can see the post.

Migrations `20260926000000`-`000003` added 10 more tables; `20260926000004` reverts them (and the `claim_due_agents` / `towns_with_unprocessed_signals` functions, now done in Python in `backend/db.py`). Where the old concepts live now: facts → `brain_runs.output.facts` (id `<run_id>:<i>`), news → `brain_runs.output.news` (not the `events` table), conversations → `agent_actions.details.lines`, consent → posting a signal, per-person visibility → `signals.value.visibility` (`full`/`vague`/`hidden`). No gift inventory.

**Conventions**
- **Assets are not in the database.** Files live in the frontend with a code manifest; the DB stores manifest keys only. Building ids (`gym`, `cafe`, `house:{user_id}`) come from the town's `tiles` + members' `house_x/house_y` (`town_map.buildings`) and match the `PLACES` keys in `town/js/layout.js`.
- **Agents act on the town hall AI's output**, not on raw signals.
- **Live movement is client-side.** `agents` rows update only when someone decides to move, not per frame. A move stores the start (`agents.x/y`), start time (`updated_at`) and `target {building_id, x, y}`; every client animates the same path from that, so late viewers can place a walker mid-route. Requires identical map, pathfinding and walk speed on all clients.
- **Access:** the backend uses the secret key (bypasses RLS) for all AI/agent writes. The frontend (publishable key) can read everything in towns it belongs to, edit its own profile, add its own signals, and accept/decline its own events. Join a town with `supabase.rpc('join_town', { code })`; creating a town auto-adds the creator. Keys live in `.env` (gitignored); see `.env.example`.
- Realtime is on for `town_members`, `agents`, `agent_actions`, `events`, `event_participants`, `friend_requests`, `town_invites` and (migration 000018) `town_clock`.
- The agent action menu is the `agent_action` enum; adding an action means a migration.
- Run backend from repo root: `py -3 -m uvicorn backend.main:app --reload` (`DISABLE_LOOPS=1` to skip the AI loops). API docs at `/docs`. Tests: `py -3 -m pytest`. Serve the web folders with `py -3 serve.py` (http://localhost:8080/frontend/).

**Town generation** (`backend/towngen/`): `POST /towns/generate {prompt, name?, me, invite_user_ids?, places?, custom_places?, landmarks?, preview?, revise?, design?}` (invitees must be friends; checked before generating, invited right after). Create flow: describe → **review** (`preview: true` returns the design unsaved; the frontend draws the whole map with `drawTown(layout, {whole: true})`) → optionally "Redo with changes" (`revise: {tiles, map, feedback}`: the model sees the built map back in legend characters via `freeform.to_rows` plus the notes, and redraws it keeping the rest; model down = the same town back) or "Try another design" → "Create this town" sends `design` (the preview's `plan`), which is laid out again with no model call and comes out identical (layout is seeded by the plan) → pick your name/color (`me`, required only to create). `places` are ids from `GET /towns/place-options` (`towngen.catalog.PLACE_TYPES`); picking any means the town has exactly those, named plainly by type ("Library"); `custom_places` keep the user's wording; `landmarks` (farm/stadium) are exact too. Frontend: "+ Create a town" on the home page. **Structure fixed, look customizable**: the model (`BRAIN_MODEL`, prompt in `towngen/prompt.py`; the user's text is data) fills a `TownPlan` (name, places, parks, decor, landmarks) and three look settings, and `towngen/layout.py` lays out the classic town (roads around a central pond park, home plots in the outer ring) so the structure always works. `landscape` = green | autumn | snowy | desert: stored as `map.landscape`; the 3D town recolours ground and trees in `town/js/models.js` `place()` (desert: sand ground, rocks and khaki scrub for trees; snowy: pale ground, pines, permanent snow cover and snow/clear weather in `sky.js`; autumn: green texture pixels recoloured orange/red/gold), and town cards (`townart.js`) use it too. `style` = city | town | suburbs | village picks the palettes (`layout.default_palettes`: suburbs and villages get only small shops and houses, never towers, even for a named place) and how full the blocks are (`building_share`); `greenery` = less | normal | lots shifts buildings vs gardens/trees. The create form has a chip row for each (`GET /towns/place-options` lists them); a pick overrides the model. `python -m backend.scripts.towngen_eval [--runs N]` checks the model reads descriptions right (a snowy village is snowy with no towers, a downtown is a city...): 30/30 on 2026-09-26, ~9s per town. Two earlier experiments let the model draw the map itself (`towngen/freeform.py`) and then compose a scene; both came out too inconsistent. Five towns made that way still exist, so `freeform.py` stays for their growth and the 3D town still draws their tiles (forest, lake, rocks, campfire, sand, bridge, docks). Drawn towns have `map.drawn = true`. If the model fails or the drawing can't be made to work, `towngen/layout.py` builds the classic symmetric city instead (`plan_source: "fallback"` when the model was down); its rules and tests (`test_towngen.py`) are unchanged. The plan and prompt are saved in `map.plan`/`map.prompt`. Joining (`admit`, and the creator on create/generate) claims the next free plot (`claim_home_slot`); when members outnumber plots, `regrow_town` grows the town: a drawn town gains a ring of its `outskirts` around the old map (roads/trails extend into it, everything shifts, old plots stay first so everyone keeps their house); the classic city is rebuilt from its plan at the next tier. Leaving frees a plot; towns don't shrink. The 3D town (`town/js/city.js`) draws roads by neighbours (straight/corner/T/cross, plain asphalt for wide areas), trails that join their neighbours, docks where a trail crosses water, forest/lake/rocks/campfire scenery, lamps along drawn roads and trails, and labels outdoor places in drawn towns; classic towns render as before.
